# IDP-Cargo · Go + DDD + Bento-Go · V2.29 integration candidate

**IDP-Cargo is one product repository and one Go module. Business code is organized by DDD bounded context; IDP-Bento-Go and IDP-Platform-Go stay separate repositories referenced as Go module versions.**

- Product module: `go.idp.xyz/idp-cargo` (candidate). The `go.idp.xyz` vanity host currently serves only `idp-bento-go`, so this path does not resolve outside the repository yet.
- Framework module: `go.idp.xyz/idp-bento-go@v0.1.0-rc.2` (release candidate, not stable), resolved from the public repository `github.com/idp-xyz/idp-bento-go`.
- Go `1.26.5`; PostgreSQL 16.

- Other contexts, transports, cross-context processes and the AI capability layer import `internal/contexts/org/api` only; the compiler rejects imports of `internal/contexts/org/internal/...` from outside ORG, and `tools/dependency-check` rejects the rest of what the layering and the Context Map do not allow.
- Further contexts (SEA, DOC, CUS, …), `internal/processes/` and `internal/ai/` get directories when their first real code lands; no empty scaffolding.
- Design documents, review reports and evidence logs are kept in the IDP-Cargo master archive and are not published in this repository.

- V2.15 implements COM V0.3, the part of the S1 business review that FUL needs first (decisions C3 and C6).
  - Order lines and obligations are measured in packs of a UN/ECE Recommendation 21 package type, gross weight and an optional volume. A line's active obligations never exceed its packs, gross weight or volume; a line is fully allocated when all its packs are.
  - `RevokeObligation` writes a new obligation version with status REVOKED. `AmendObligation` revokes one obligation and allocates its replacement in one command. `CancelCustomerOrder` revokes what is still active and cancels the order for good.
  - FUL reserves an obligation version for a shipment, and releases it, through ObligationReservation v1 (`ReserveObligationForFulfillment`, `ReleaseObligationReservation`). While a shipment holds an obligation, COM refuses to revoke it or to cancel its order. COM never queries FUL.
  - `GetObligationSnapshotV2` adds packs, package type, gross weight, volume, the version's status, the latest version and the holding shipment. ObligationSnapshot v1 stays published, with the packs as a Recommendation 20 package unit (`XPX` for pallets).
  - Migration `000002_com_obligation_lifecycle` replaces the line and obligation quantities, adds cancellation and `obligation_reservation`, and replaces the triggers. It refuses to run on a `cargo_com` that already holds V0.1 orders. The triggers lock the order row, so a reservation and a revocation of one obligation never both commit; PG16 tests race them through the api and in raw SQL. A model-based property test runs random sequences of all six commands.
- V2.15 also adds FUL, step 4 of the S1 slice: the shipment plan (FUL tactical candidate V0.1).
  - A shipment plans exact COM obligation versions of one customer party and legal entity. COM must report each version ACTIVE, the obligation's latest, and reserved for the shipment; FUL asks before its own transaction. The shipment has its own identity, never the order's.
  - Every change writes an immutable plan version: transport mode, origin and destination UN/LOCODEs, departure window, and the cargo scope summed from the planned obligations. Confirmation needs a complete route and re-checks every obligation with COM; a confirmed plan that changes must be confirmed again.
  - Releasing an obligation or cancelling the shipment comes first; the caller then releases COM's reservation. FUL never writes to COM and never asserts loading or delivery.
  - `GetShipmentPlanSnapshot` returns ShipmentPlanSnapshot v1 for BOOK in step 5. The kernel gains `LocationCode`, which keeps UN/LOCODE and IATA airport codes apart.

PG16 tests race eight shipments for one obligation and eight amendments against one revision; a test in `internal/bootstrap` runs ORG, RATE, COM and FUL through the C6 handshake.
- V2.16 implements owner decision F1 (FUL tactical candidate V0.2): a shipment plans a part of an obligation version, and the parts of one obligation may be planned in several shipments. A carrier's shortload or a rollover is replanned in FUL, without a COM amendment.

A quote totals in its own currency at frozen exchange rates, one per other currency, with source, date and the ISO 4217 minor digits used; lines convert one by one, halves away from zero. The margin floor is checked in the quote currency. The rates price the quote; invoicing rates belong to FIN.

R4: `ExpireQuote` ends a quote not accepted once its validity has passed (the scheduled job follows in S2); expired quotes are frozen.   - T1: `kernel.RecordWindow` admits a business time up to seven days before the time RATE records the command (configurable with `WithRecordWindow`) and five minutes after it; the acceptance's recording time is kept. A command committed earlier still replays later.
  - C8: optional Incoterms rule, edition (2010 or 2020), named place and service level on the quote.
  - `GetQuoteSnapshotV2` returns all of it, without costs or margins; v1 keeps each line's own currency and totals in the quote currency.
  - Migration `rate/000002_rate_quote_v03` converts V0.2 quotes without loss: lines take the quote currency, recipients are customers, and accepted quotes get the evidence kind UNRECORDED and no recording time. New table `cargo_rate.quote_exchange_rate`, immutable.

A line never falls below what its active obligations commit, and keeps its package type and units while it has any. A difference measured at receipt is not a correction; FUL records it.

COM locks the quote before it counts, and the database checks both again.

New tables `cargo_com.order_line_amendment` and `cargo_com.order_policy`, immutable.
- V2.22 makes the database check an order's status (COM V0.5.1). The status is derived from the order's lines and obligations: CANCELLED once cancelled, RECORDED while no obligation is active, ALLOCATED when every line's active obligations take all its packs, otherwise PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED. COM writes it with every change; until now a write past COM, such as a revocation written in SQL, could leave a status COM then refused to restore. Deferred constraint triggers now check every order a transaction touches when it commits. Migration `com/000005_com_order_status_derived` derives stale statuses again; status is derived data, so nothing is lost.
- V2.23 implements ORG V0.3: ORG keeps each party's addresses and contacts (decision C9 as amended on 2026-11-09, privacy assessment V1.1), so COM V0.6 can carry them onto orders by role.
  - An address has a short code unique among the party's active addresses, purposes (OFFICE for documents and the office, PICKUP, DELIVERY, BILLING), the name and up to four lines as printed on documents, city, state or province, postcode and country, optionally a UN/LOCODE in that country, a residential flag, and optionally the same address in a local language such as Chinese. A contact has purposes (BOOKING, DOCUMENTS, ACCOUNTS), a name and at least one of phone, mobile and e-mail, and nothing else about the person.
  - Each purpose has at most one default address and one default contact. An address or contact that is deactivated, erased or loses a purpose stops being its default.
  - The address book is an aggregate of its own with its own revision; the party's identity revision, which PartyRef carries, does not change. Every command is one revision with a change record (who, why, when); every change of an address or contact is a new immutable version, and defaults are kept per revision. Changes take effect when ORG records them.
  - Commands `AddPartyAddress`, `ChangePartyAddress`, `DeactivatePartyAddress`, `SetDefaultPartyAddress`, `AddPartyContact`, `ChangePartyContact`, `DeactivatePartyContact`, `ErasePartyContact`, `SetDefaultPartyContact`; queries `GetPartyAddresses`, `GetPartyAddress` (any version) and `GetPartyContacts`. Reading contacts needs its own authorization (`ReadPartyContacts`). Events carry identifiers only. An archived party's address book is closed.
  - Erasing a contact clears its name, phone, mobile and e-mail in every version and keeps purposes, statuses and the change records.
  - Migration `org/000007_org_party_address_book` replaces the encrypted `party_address` and `party_contact` candidates of migration 000001, which no code ever wrote, and refuses to run if they hold rows. Versions, defaults and change records cannot be rewritten; a contact version can only be erased. Deferred constraint triggers check every address book a transaction touches when it commits. The application role gets only the grants it needs on the new tables.
  - PG16 tests race eight commands on one address book revision (one commits) and show that the database refuses eight kinds of writes past ORG; a model-based property test runs random sequences of all nine commands.

  - A party has typed identifiers (MD-06): `CN_USCC`, the 18-character unified social credit code checked against the GB 32100 check character; `CN_CUSTOMS`, the 10-character customs code; `AEO` (issuing country and code, as declarations print it); `EU_EORI`; `US_EIN`; and `REGISTRATION` for other national registration codes. Each entry has its issuing country and evidence; one active entry per type and country; an entry may record that the party has none (declarations then print NO). A code belongs to one party of a tenant; recording it for a second party is a conflict that names the holder, to be merged. Retiring keeps the entry as history; a correction retires and records again.
  - A party has an optional English name, printable ASCII, as international documents print it; the legal name stays the registered one.
  - Identity is part of the party, so `RecordPartyIdentifier`, `RetirePartyIdentifier` and `SetPartyEnglishName` advance the revision PartyRef carries; `GetPartyIdentity` returns names, status, identifiers and revision. `ResolvePartyRole` now returns the English name and identifiers in the same view as the revision, for COM V0.6 to freeze. Events name the identifier's type and country, never the code.
  - An address may carry the organization's phone and e-mail at that address, such as a switchboard (manifests require the shipper's phone); a residential address carries neither.
  - Migration `org/000008_org_party_identifiers` replaces the encrypted `party_identifier` candidate of migration 000001, which no code ever wrote, and refuses to run if it holds rows. The database checks each code's form, including the GB 32100 check character (`cargo_org.uscc_valid`), one active entry per type and country, one active holder per code, and lets an identifier only be retired, once. The application role gets INSERT and the retirement columns only.
  - PG16 tests race eight parties recording one code (one commits), and show the database refusing a wrong check character, a second active entry, rewrites and deletions of identifiers, an undone retirement and a non-ASCII English name.
- V2.25 implements COM V0.6: the order side of C9, with the decisions C11–C14 of 2026-11-10 (CargoWise practice, delegated by the owner).
  - An order may carry the requested transport mode (AIR, SEA, ROAD, RAIL) and movement type (door-to-door, door-to-port, port-to-door, port-to-port), as CargoWise shipments carry a transport mode and pickup and delivery modes (C11). `ChangeOrderTransport` records the next version with a reason.
  - Per role, the order freezes its shipper, consignee and notify party: the ORG party at its revision with legal and English names, registration country and identifiers (ORG V0.4), and a documentary address. The shipper and the consignee must hold that role in ORG for the quote's legal entity (C9); the notify party need only be active (C13). The consignee of a SEA order may be TO ORDER or TO ORDER OF a holder, as text, with a notify party; an AIR order needs a named consignee (C11).
  - Addresses come from ORG: the party's default OFFICE address, or another active address it names. A movement that starts at a door carries the shipper's PICKUP address, one that ends at a door the consignee's DELIVERY address (C14). Without a default the order must name or give one. An address given on the order records who gave it, why, and the ORG address it replaced; nothing is written back to ORG.
  - A contact is optional: by default the party's DOCUMENTS contact, read from ORG with the caller's own authorization. Contacts are stored apart from the order, never in events or receipts, and read through `GetOrderParties` only by callers authorized for `ReadOrderContacts`.
  - `RecordCustomerOrder` takes the transport and parties; `SetOrderParty` sets or replaces one role as a new version, with a reason when it replaces, until the order is cancelled (C12). Each version publishes `OrderPartySet.v1` with the role, version, kind and whether it carries anything given on the order: the trigger later screening needs (X1). ORG changes never alter a frozen order.
  - Migration `com/000006_com_order_parties` adds `order_transport`, `order_party` and `order_party_contact`. Versions are immutable; a frozen contact can only be erased. Deferred constraint triggers check at commit that versions are numbered from 1, contact rows match, and the current parties keep the rules of C9, C11 and C14. Orders recorded before V0.6 have neither transport nor parties.
  - A PG16 test runs ORG, RATE and COM together through defaults, door pickup, a delivery override, a TO ORDER consignee and a refused change to AIR, contact authorization, events without personal data, and writes past COM refused by privileges, triggers and the commit check.
- V2.26 implements FUL V0.3: decision F2 (option 4), T1 in FUL, and the check of C11 against COM's requested transport mode.
  - Location reference data lives where the repository structure baseline puts it: `internal/reference` (contract `internal/reference/api`, implementation `internal/reference/locations`) and schema `cargo_ref`, read-only for the application. Editions never change and one is current. Defects found by real runs and fixed:

1. `go.sum` held only the two framework lines, so every Bento build failed with `missing go.sum entry`. `go.mod` / `go.sum` are now the complete graph from Go 1.26.5 `go mod tidy`.
2. `cargo-migrate` did not create the Bento schema, so the first framework migration failed with `schema "bento" does not exist`. Bento migrations never run `CREATE SCHEMA`; that is the consumer's first migration step (bento-go `docs/design/postgres-adapter-contract.md`).
3. `PartyStore.Save` hashed nanosecond validity times into `role_id`, while `timestamptz` keeps microseconds. After a reload the ID changed and the re-insert of an existing role violated `party_role_approved_no_overlap` (23P01). The hash and the insert now use microsecond-truncated UTC times.
4. `PartyStore.Load` read the party row and its roles in two statements. Under READ COMMITTED each statement takes a new snapshot, so a command committing in between produced a torn aggregate (old revision, new role) and a losing handler failed with `ErrRoleOverlap` instead of a revision conflict. `Load` now locks the root row with `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`.
5. RATE's anticorruption layer wrapped ORG's error chain with `%w`. ### The HTTP API

`cmd/cargo-api` serves `/v1`. Until the IDP identity platform is connected, a bearer token is a development token: HS256-signed with `IDP_CARGO_API_TOKEN_KEY` (base64 of 32 bytes or more), naming its issuer and audience (`-token-issuer`, `-token-audience`), the tenant, the actor and the permissions (`<context>:<Action>`, `<context>:*` or `*`; queries need `<context>:Read`), valid for a day at most. Whoever holds the key can sign any tenant and permission, so the server starts only with `-dev-tokens`, never in production. Tenant and actor always come from the token, never from a body. Use `-addr 127.0.0.1:0` locally: the server takes a free port and logs it.

```sh
export IDP_CARGO_API_TOKEN_KEY=$(head -c 32 /dev/urandom | base64)
go run ./cmd/cargo-api -mint-token -tenant t1 -actor pricing-1 -permissions rate:Read,rate:MaintainChargeCodes
IDP_CARGO_POSTGRES_DSN=... go run ./cmd/cargo-api -dev-tokens -addr 127.0.0.1:0
```

- A command is a `POST` with an `Idempotency-Key` (its command id: sent again, it answers its first receipt; with another body, 409 `IDEMPOTENCY_MISMATCH`), `If-Match: "n"` when it changes an existing version (428 without it, 409 `VERSION_CONFLICT` when stale), and `occurred_at`, its business time, in the body: a retry sends the same body. It answers a receipt (`command_id`, `subject_id`, `new_revision`) with `ETag: "n"`, 201 and `Location` for a create.
- Errors are `application/problem+json` (RFC 9457) with `code` (a business code such as `RATE_VERSION_OVERLAP` where the context names one) and `correlation_id`, from the context's error kinds only: 400, 401, 403, 404, 405, 409, 413, 415, 422, 428, 503 `COMMIT_UNCERTAIN` (send the request again with the same key), 500 with nothing described.
- `GET /healthz` and `GET /readyz` (the database) take no token. `X-Correlation-ID` is taken when well formed, otherwise made, and returned.

### Local PostgreSQL 16 on IDP-Cargo's ports

Ports 25430-25439 are reserved for IDP-Cargo on developer machines: 25432 for integration runs (`dev`), 25433 for upgrade runs (`upgrade`), 25434 for empty-database checks (`empty`), the rest reserved. They lie below the default ephemeral port ranges of Linux and Windows. `tools/localpg/localpg.sh` serves each port only from its own labelled container (`idp-cargo-pg16-<role>`, PostgreSQL 16.15) and refuses to start, print a DSN or stop anything when a port is held by another program, so tests and migrations never reach someone else's server.

```sh
tools/localpg/localpg.sh up dev            # start, or confirm, idp-cargo-pg16-dev on 127.0.0.1:25432
eval "$(tools/localpg/localpg.sh env dev)" # IDP_CARGO_MIGRATOR_DSN and IDP_CARGO_POSTGRES_DSN, as CI sets them
go run ./cmd/cargo-migrate -manifest migrations/manifest/contexts.txt -apply
tools/localpg/localpg.sh grants dev        # the application role and the grants of the CI workflow
go test -tags integration -count=1 ./...
tools/localpg/localpg.sh down dev          # remove the container and its data
```

## Important boundaries

- The approval actor and tenant identity are **not trusted merely because they are inside a Go struct**. The HTTP adapters take them from the verified token's principal; the development tokens of `cmd/cargo-api` are not an identity platform, and permissions in a token stand in for the IDP policy service until it is connected.
- `app.tenant_id` RLS protects against accidental missing/wrong query scopes. SQL injection that can execute arbitrary `set_config` is out of its security boundary.
- A missing or legacy `result_revision=0` receipt does **not** count as execution success.
- Callers branch on the `api` error kinds (`ErrConflict`, `ErrRejected`, `ErrUnauthorized`, …), never on ORG or Bento causes; `org.ErrConflict` and Bento `repository.ErrConflict` both classify as `api.ErrConflict`.
- No direct business writes across Cargo bounded contexts.
- `internal/bootstrap` gives every context its own Bento transaction owner. A context that calls another context inside its own transaction gets `application.ErrTransactionConflict`, so cross-context queries run before the caller opens its transaction.
- `EventID` is not a substitute for CommandID deduplication. No Exactly-Once guarantee.
- The relay delivers at least once: a crash between publish and settle re-delivers after the lease expires. Sinks and consumers deduplicate by event ID, which the webhook sends as `Idempotency-Key`. Leases are wall-clock timestamps, so worker hosts need synchronized clocks.
- RATE keeps customers as ORG party references only; legal names stay in ORG. Quote costs and margins never leave RATE.
- RATE's rules follow the 2026-11-06 business review and the owner decisions of 2026-11-09; the margin floor is supplied by `MarginPolicy`, not hard-coded. Approval for non-standard payment terms and non-functional currencies (R2) waits for their reference sources, and rate cards (R6) for S2.
- COM keeps the customer's legal name and country only as the transaction snapshot frozen with the order (MD-05). The obligation snapshots carry identifiers, packs, weight and volume, not names, because FUL does not consume legal names.
- The reservation is COM's own fact, set and cleared through COM's api, so the COM → FUL relationship keeps its direction. In S1 an API client drives the handshake (rule T6); a release that FUL has not recorded first is caught by FUL's own re-checks, not by COM.
- COM's rules follow the 2026-11-06 business review (C3–C7), the owner decisions of 2026-11-09 (C1, C2, C8, C9 as amended, C10) and the decisions C11–C14 of 2026-11-10, taken by the assessor on the owner's delegation to follow CargoWise practice. Once an order is closed or cancelled its contacts are restricted and kept until the retention period ends, then erased (P1; the period is to be confirmed by legal counsel). Package type codes and weight and volume units are checked for form only until reference data exists (MD-25).
- FUL's rules are a candidate design pending business review: one transport mode per shipment (AIR, SEA, ROAD or RAIL), additions only while the plan is a draft, one weight unit per shipment, and volume declared by all of its obligations or by none. Splitting obligations into parts follows owner decision F1; locations follow F2 option 4 (UN/LOCODEs for every mode, airport-level locations with an IATA code for AIR, IATA airport codes accepted as input); a shipment's mode must be the one its orders request (C11). The customer's requested origin, destination and dates are not on the order yet, so planners enter them in FUL.
- The location reference data of S1 is a subset of the UNECE UN/LOCODE release of 2026-11-01 for the first lines (M1). Its IATA airport codes and the aliases carriers use (CNSHA, CNNGB, CNTAO and CNXMN for the ports of Shanghai, Ningbo, Qingdao and Xiamen) are the assessor's compilation and must be verified against IATA and carrier data, or replaced by the full import, before the pilot (E3).
- ORG keeps the official codes of organizations (unified social credit code, customs code, AEO, EORI, EIN, registration numbers); these are public registration data, not personal data, and leave ORG only through its queries, never in events. Identifiers of natural persons (identity cards, passports) are not accepted.
- ORG keeps addresses and contacts under privacy assessment V1.1 (an assessor's opinion awaiting the data protection owner's sign-off). Contacts are personal data: reading them is authorized separately, they never appear in events or receipts, and they can be erased. Contact fields are stored in plaintext under row level security; encryption at rest waits for KMS. Development and tests use simulated data only (M1); real personal data needs the steps of section 6 of the assessment before the pilot (E3).
- The earliest ORG master data cannot be signed off until a Cargo business owner validates authoritative identity, legal status and partner role semantics.
